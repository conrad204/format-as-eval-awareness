**Figure.** Real, hooked, full-layer interchange-patching (activation-swap) causal
test on Llama-3.1-8B, rank 16 -- not frozen-activation geometry, not a synthetic
steering vector: at every layer, the projection onto `Q_format(16)` is swapped
between matched real benchmark/casual counterparts (same payload block, same
purpose), the real bf16 model runs forward for real from that layer, and
purpose/format probes read out the final-layer hidden state.

**Left:** format interchange-intervention accuracy -- does the format readout
move, in sign, toward the swapped-in counterpart's own baseline format score.
`real_swap` climbs from ~0.54 to ~0.98 with depth; `random_control` (same real
content, unmatched random rank-16 subspace) stays at chance until very late
layers. But this comparison understates both effects: a random rank-16
projection captures only ~16/4096 = 0.4% of a vector's energy on average, so
it's a much smaller intervention. `matched_control` (fresh random direction,
magnitude-matched per row to that row's own `real_swap` delta norm) is the
fair comparison, and `real_swap` still clearly beats it from mid-depth onward
(0.93 vs 0.65 at 63% depth) -- `Q_format(16)` is not just a bigger perturbation,
it is a more effective one at causally moving the format readout.

**Right:** mean absolute purpose-readout shift (patched vs. unpatched
baseline). `random_control` stays flat and small throughout (~0.3-0.6) --
consistent with its tiny average perturbation size. `real_swap` and
`matched_control` both grow with depth since they inject comparable energy,
but their relationship inverts partway through: `real_swap` disrupts purpose
*more* than `matched_control` through the first ~55% of depth, then *less*
from ~60% onward, ending at 0.84 vs. 5.83 at the final layer -- a same-size
random push is roughly 7x more disruptive to purpose than the structured
`Q_format(16)` swap by the end of the network.

**Reading:** `Q_format(16)` is a functionally distinguished direction, not
just a correlated one -- it causally moves the format readout more reliably
than an equally-sized random perturbation, and at deep layers it is
comparatively *protective* of the purpose readout relative to a same-size
generic push. This does not mean the swap is purpose-neutral (it still moves
purpose substantially, especially at early-to-mid depth) -- see
`results/full320_v3_qb_c_leakage_diagnostic.json` for evidence that
`Q_format(16)` resolves a real, depth-growing share of the purpose x format
interaction term `C`, which plausibly explains the early-to-mid-depth
disruption.

**Caveat, same as everywhere else in this project:** this is a descriptive
full-layer trajectory, not a per-layer significance test -- adjacent layers
share almost all their variance (residual-stream continuity), so no per-layer
significance count or cross-layer correction is computed or implied here.
